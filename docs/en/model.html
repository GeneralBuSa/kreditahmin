How does the model work?

KrediTahmin runs on a Gradient Boosting model. While training it I compared 10 classification algorithms; the biggest gain came not from the choice of algorithm but from new variables I derived from the data.

Impact of feature engineering

Banks look at ratios more than raw amounts. I derived four ratios from the raw columns: total assets, loan / income, loan / assets and, most importantly, annual payment / annual income. The chart shows each model's 5-fold cross-validation accuracy without and with these variables.

Without new variables With new variables
  • Gradient Boosting 99.9%
  • Decision Tree 99.9%
  • LightGBM 99.9%
  • AdaBoost 99.9%
  • Random Forest 99.8%
  • XGBoost 99.8%
  • SVM 95.4%
  • Logistic Regression 93.6%

  • Naive Bayes 62.7%

Every tree-based model rose above 99%. Naive Bayes dropped instead: it assumes the variables are independent of each other, while the derived ratios are strongly correlated with the existing columns.

What does the model look at?

Feature importances of the final model. The credit score drives most of the decision and the annual payment / income ratio most of the rest; income, assets, education and employment have almost no effect.

  • Credit score 84.3%
  • Annual payment / annual income 12.8%
  • Loan / total assets 1.6%
  • Loan / annual income 1.0%
  • Loan term 0.3%

How it was built

  1. Data cleaning. Whitespace in column names and values was removed; 28 negative residential asset values were set to 0. There were no missing or duplicate records.
  2. Exploratory analysis. A sharp threshold at a credit score of 550 was found. Education, employment and family size have no effect on the approval rate.
  3. Feature engineering. Four ratios were derived. The best model's error dropped about 20-fold.
  4. Model comparison. Logistic Regression, SVM, Naive Bayes, KNN, Decision Tree, Random Forest, AdaBoost, Gradient Boosting, XGBoost and LightGBM were tried.
  5. Transformation. Yeo-Johnson was tried; it was not used because it didn't improve the linear model.
  6. Hyperparameter tuning. The top 6 models were tuned with RandomizedSearchCV; Gradient Boosting, with the highest cross-validation score, was chosen.
  7. Deployment. The model was saved with pickle and connected to this Flask app.

Good to know

  • Amounts in the dataset are in Indian rupees (₹). The credit score follows India's CIBIL system (300–900), a different scale from other credit scores such as Turkey's Findeks (1–1900).
  • The rules in the data are too sharp to occur in real bank data; it is most likely synthetic. The 99.8% accuracy is specific to this dataset.
  • For applications with a low credit score, the model associates a high payment / income ratio with approval. This pattern comes from the data and does not reflect real banking logic.
  • The app shows a warning when a value falls outside the range of the training data; predictions in that region are not reliable.
